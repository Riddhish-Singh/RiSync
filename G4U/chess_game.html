Chess

The Royal Game

How to Play Chess

♟ The Objective

Chess is a two-player strategy game. You play as White, the AI plays as Black. The goal is to put the opponent's King in Checkmate — a position where the King is attacked and cannot escape. White always moves first.

♟ The Pieces

♔
King
Moves 1 square in any direction. Must be protected at all times.
♕
Queen
Most powerful piece. Moves any number of squares in any direction.
♖
Rook
Moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
♗
Bishop
Moves any number of squares diagonally. Stays on one color.
♘
Knight
Moves in an L-shape: 2 squares one way, 1 square the other. Can jump over pieces.
♙
Pawn
Moves 1 square forward, 2 on first move. Captures diagonally. Promotes on the last rank.

♟ Core Rules

1
Taking turns — White moves first, then Black, alternating every turn.
2
Capturing — Move your piece to a square occupied by an enemy piece to capture and remove it.
3
Check — When the King is directly attacked, it is in check. You must resolve check immediately.
4
Checkmate — The King is in check and has no way to escape. The attacking side wins.
5
Stalemate — If a player has no legal moves but is NOT in check, the game is a draw.
6
Castling — A special move: the King moves 2 squares toward a Rook, and the Rook jumps over to the other side. Neither can have moved before, and the King cannot be in check.
7
En Passant — If a pawn moves 2 squares on its first move, an enemy pawn beside it may capture it as if it had moved only 1 square. Must be done immediately.
8
Promotion — When a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board, it promotes and becomes a Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight.

♟ Strategy Tips

• Control the center — Placing pawns and pieces in the center (e4, d4, e5, d5) gives you more room to operate.

• Develop your pieces early — Move Knights and Bishops out before attacking.

• Keep your King safe — Castle early to tuck the King away behind pawns.

• Think before you move — Ask: does my move leave any piece unprotected? Does it create an opportunity for the opponent?
